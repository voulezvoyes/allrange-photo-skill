## Photographic Realism Policy (v1.1)

This policy overrides any conflicting aesthetic guidance elsewhere in this skill. Prioritize photographic credibility over visual beauty.

### Required image characteristics
- Authentic commercial documentary photography, not digital illustration or stylized wellness imagery.
- Physically plausible directional daylight, realistic shadows, and neutral white balance.
- Natural, distinct textures in skin, hair, clothing, furniture, plants, and architecture.
- Realistic 35mm or 50mm camera perspective and moderate depth of field.
- Ordinary, believable environments and candid human behavior.
- Preserve fine material detail instead of smoothing or blending surfaces.

### Avoid
- Painterly, watercolor, oil-paint, CGI, or rendered appearance.
- Waxy skin, beauty filters, excessive smoothing, artificial sharpening.
- Heavy beige/orange color grading, dreamy haze, bloom, HDR, excessive bokeh.
- Repetitive stock-photo smiles, overly staged poses, unrealistically pristine interiors.
- Synthetic grain used to conceal poor image detail.

### Composition rules
- Vary camera angle, subject distance, framing, and subject position naturally.
- Do not default to the same 45-degree angle or composition.
- Add intentional copy space only when the requested website layout needs it.
- Do not sacrifice environmental realism to create an excessively large blank wall or empty background.

### AllrangeKit healthcare integrity
- Depict fictional adults in respectful, everyday situations.
- Do not fabricate visible test results, medical interfaces, kit packaging, logos, or medical claims.
- Do not represent fictional subjects as real patients, testimonials, or staff.

### Output and quality check
- Preserve the existing Korean user-input template and camera controls.
- Translate Korean scene descriptions to precise English photographic instructions.
- Include the realism requirements in the final image prompt.
- Check for painterly textures, incoherent object details, anatomy, lighting, and materials.
- If image inspection is available, identify failures and recommend regeneration.
- Never claim an image has passed visual inspection without actually inspecting it.
